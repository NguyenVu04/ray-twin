3. Overview

A self-organizing network (SON) changes the configuration of a mobile network automatically. Typical changes are the antenna tilt, the transmit power and the handover settings. Before SON applies a change, it must know how coverage, interference and service quality will change. A test on the live network is slow and has operational risk. Each wrong change can affect the customers in the area.

RayTwin is the radio-propagation core of a digital twin, that is, a numerical model of the real network. RayTwin uses a 3D model of the area and traces radio rays through it. It calculates how strong the signal is at each point, and which paths the signal takes. SON tests each candidate configuration on the twin first. Only the best candidate goes to the live network. Measurement reports from phones (MDT) calibrate the twin, so that its predictions agree with the real network.

Network planning engineers, SON researchers and calibration researchers use RayTwin. A service operator runs RayTwin on many machines when one machine is too slow. In releases 0.1 to 0.3, RayTwin has no graphical user interface. Each person uses Python code, a Jupyter notebook or a command line.

Positioning statement For network planning engineers and SON researchers who must know the effect of a configuration change before they apply it, RayTwin is a ray-tracing engine for digital twins that calculates versioned 3D radio maps, channel responses and service-quality estimates from a 3D model of the area. Unlike a test on the live network, it gives the answer without operational risk, and the same inputs always give the same result.

4. Key concepts

This section explains the ideas that the use cases depend on. Each subsection has a diagram. Appendix A gives the formulas, with every symbol defined and a numerical example. Links in the form EQ-nn go to these formulas.

4.1 The SON loop and the digital twin

A SON application asks questions such as: “If the tilt of cell 17 increases by 2°, how do coverage and interference change?” RayTwin answers these questions on a digital twin. Figure 1 shows the loop:

  1. The live network sends measurement data: MDT reports from phones, performance counters (PM) and the configuration (CM).
  2. The measurement data calibrates the twin.
  3. SON sends candidate configurations to the twin. The twin returns the predicted key performance indicators (KPIs).
  4. Only the selected configuration goes to the network management system, which applies it to the live network.

For SON, a stable result is as important as an accurate result. When SON compares two tilt values, random simulation noise can be larger than the difference that SON must measure. Section 4.5 tells how RayTwin removes this problem.

4.2 How ray tracing estimates the radio signal

A radio wave spreads from the antenna in all directions. When the objects in the area are much larger than the wavelength, the wave behaves almost like a set of straight rays. At 3.5 GHz, the wavelength is approximately 8.6 cm. This is much smaller than a building, so RayTwin can use rays.

RayTwin uses the method of shooting and bouncing rays (SBR). Figure 2 shows the loop for one ray:

  1. RayTwin launches many rays from each transmitter, in directions that cover the full sphere evenly. Each ray carries an equal part of the transmit power, multiplied by the antenna gain in its direction.
  2. RayTwin finds the first surface that the ray hits. A tree of nested boxes, the bounding volume hierarchy (BVH), makes this search fast.
  3. The ray adds its power to each voxel that it crosses. A voxel is one small box of the 3D grid of the radio map.
  4. At the hit point, RayTwin reads the material. It then selects reflection or transmission at random. A stronger interaction has a higher probability.
  5. The ray continues until it reaches the maximum number of bounces, or until its power is too small.

In releases 0.1 to 0.3, a ray can travel in line of sight (LoS). It can reflect like a mirror (specular reflection). It can also go through a thin wall (transmission). RayTwin does not model diffraction around edges or diffuse scattering from rough surfaces yet. For this reason, RayTwin can give a signal that is too low deep behind buildings (constraint C-13).

SBR is a Monte Carlo method. It uses random choices, so its result has noise. More rays give less noise. The noise in one voxel is approximately 1/√n, where n is the number of ray segments that cross the voxel (EQ-07). A voxel that at least 100 segments cross has a noise of approximately 0.4 dB. This document calls it a ray-sufficient voxel. Every comparison and every mobility KPI uses only ray-sufficient voxels (BR-01).

4.3 The two results: radio map and channel impulse response

RayTwin gives two kinds of results. They answer different questions.

ResultWhat it containsWhat it answersWhere RayTwin calculates it
3D radio map The average path gain in each voxel of a 3D grid, for each transmitter and each band. The default voxel size is 5 m. RSRP and SINR come from it when a reader asks for them Coverage, interference, best server, load balancing In the full volume of the area
Channel impulse response (CIR) per antenna element A list of paths from each transmitter antenna element to each receiver antenna element. Each path has a complex amplitude and a delay Beam selection, delay spread, precoding, fading Only at the receiver points that the actor gives. One link with a 64T64R transmitter, a UE with 4 antennas and 100 paths needs approximately 200 KB for each band

How RayTwin calculates the radio map. Each ray adds its power, multiplied by the length of its segment inside the voxel (Figure 3). The sum gives the average power density in the voxel. This is the track-length estimator. In free space, it gives the same value as the standard free-space formula (EQ-08).

How RayTwin calculates the CIR. SBR shows approximately which surfaces a path hits. The image method then finds the exact path. It mirrors the transmitter across the wall and draws a straight line from the mirror image to the receiver (Figure 4, EQ-10). The CIR keeps the phase of each path. Thus it shows deep fading dips, which an average-power radio map cannot show (EQ-11).

4.4 Versioned inputs: snapshot, manifest and RAN config

RayTwin keeps every input of a calculation in one immutable set, the snapshot. A JSON file, the manifest, lists the inputs. They are the scene, the material field, the bands, the transmitters with their antenna arrays, and the solver settings with the random seed. The snapshot id is a hash of the manifest content. The same content gives the same id, and the same id gives the same result (BR-02). The storage location is not part of the id. Thus one scene on local disk, on MinIO or on S3 has the same id.

Some radio parameters act only when RayTwin reads a result. They are the reference-signal power per resource element (EPRE), the cell on/off state, the noise figure, and the UE gain and body loss. From release 0.2, the cell individual offset (CIO) and the handover parameters are also in this group. RayTwin keeps these parameters in a separate document, the RAN config, with its own hash. A change to the RAN config does not change the snapshot, and it needs no new ray trace (BR-04).

RayTwin never overwrites stored data. Each write creates a new object (BR-03). When calibration makes a new material field, RayTwin stores a new field version. The old versions stay readable.

Figure 6 tells which changes need a new ray trace. Use it before you plan a study: a change that needs no new trace costs little.

4.5 Same seed, same result

RayTwin gives bit-identical results for the same snapshot on the same backend (NFR-03). The number of CPU threads, the number of workers, the batch size and the merge order do not change the result. Two design rules make this possible:

  • Each random number comes from a key and a counter: the manifest seed, the transmitter, the ray index and the bounce. The key does not contain the job or the thread.
  • Each backend adds the power contributions as 64-bit integers. An integer sum is the same in any order. A floating-point sum is not.

The job is not part of the key. Thus two jobs that SON compares use the same random numbers. This is the method of common random numbers. For example, two jobs that differ only in tilt trace the same ray paths. Only the ray weights differ (NFR-05). The difference between the two results then comes from the configuration, not from noise. A UE scenario of release 0.2 uses the same rule (NFR-13).

4.6 Library mode and distributed mode

RayTwin runs in two modes. Both modes use the same physics core, so they give the same result (NFR-06).

  • Library mode. RayTwin runs inside the Python process of the actor, on the CPU or the GPU of that machine. It needs no other service. Scenes can come from local disk. This mode suits research, notebooks and continuous integration (CI).
  • Distributed mode. A coordinator splits a job into tasks. Workers on one or more machines pull the tasks, calculate them and write the partial results (parts) to an object store. The coordinator merges the parts. The client talks to the coordinator through NATS, a messaging system. In release 0.1, one Docker Compose command starts NATS, MinIO, the coordinator and N workers.

Figure 9 shows the steps of one radio-map job in distributed mode. Messages carry only references. Large data always goes through the object store. A worker confirms each finished task. If a worker stops before it confirms, the message system gives the task to another worker after a timeout (30 s by default). The second worker calculates the same result, because the result depends only on the seed (NFR-07).

4.7 Compute backends

RayTwin has three backends: CPU, GPU compute shader and GPU ray query. In automatic mode, RayTwin selects the first available backend in the order of Figure 10. When it skips a backend, it records the reason (FR-10). The actor can also force one backend in the configuration.

  • GPU ray query uses the ray-tracing cores (RT cores) of the GPU. It needs a Vulkan GPU with the ray-query feature.
  • GPU compute shader searches the BVH in a general GPU program. It needs a Vulkan GPU with 64-bit floating point and 64-bit atomic operations.
  • CPU is the reference. It runs on every supported system, also on macOS.

In release 0.1, the GPU backends run on Linux and Windows through Vulkan. On macOS, RayTwin uses the CPU. Each GPU backend gives bit-identical results between runs and stays within 10−4 (relative) of the CPU (NFR-04). RayTwin selects the ray batch size from the free GPU memory. Thus a GPU with 4 GB is sufficient for the munich sample scene (NFR-09).

4.8 One trace, many bands

The path of a ray does not depend on the frequency. Thus one ray trace can serve several frequency bands. For each band, RayTwin calculates only the material response, the antenna pattern and the wavelength again (Figure 11). The radio map has a band axis: [band, transmitter, z, y, x].

RayTwin selects reflection or transmission at one reference frequency. It then corrects the power of each other band with a weight, so that the estimate stays correct on average (EQ-09). Release 0.1 validates the FR1 bands (410 MHz to 7125 MHz). The parameters of most building materials are valid only from 1 GHz. Outside the valid range of a material, RayTwin gives a warning (FR-11).

4.9 Material field and calibration

The material of a surface decides how much power reflects and how much goes through (EQ-04, EQ-05). RayTwin describes materials as a field over the area, not as one fixed value for each object. The field has two parts:

  • A prior. Each surface has a material class, for example brick, glass or concrete. The recommendation ITU-R P.2040 gives the electrical parameters of each class as a function of frequency.
  • A correction δ. RayTwin keeps δ on a sparse 3D grid with a step of 2 m to 5 m. It interpolates δ at each hit point. With δ = 0, the material is the ITU material of its class exactly (EQ-06).

Calibration finds the δ values that make the predicted RSRP agree with the RSRP in the MDT reports. Figure 12 shows the loop. RayTwin traces the rays one time and records the paths to each measurement point in a tape (frozen paths). PyTorch then changes δ in small steps and calculates the RSRP again from the tape, without a new trace. The paths can depend on the materials, so RayTwin traces again after a set number of steps. The result is a new version of the material field (EQ-15).

Calibration has a known risk. RSRP has no phase, and many MDT samples have a poor position. The correction can then absorb errors of the geometry or of the network configuration. A field that fits the current configuration can give wrong values after a tilt change. This is exactly the type of question that SON asks. For this reason, RayTwin checks calibration on cells and tilts that calibration did not use (BR-08, EQ-16).

Release 0.1 has no real MDT. It checks calibration with a twin experiment (Figure 13). A known “true” material field produces simulated MDT through the reference simulator, Sionna RT 2.2.0. RayTwin then calibrates from that data, and the result is compared with the truth. A different simulator makes the data, so the test does not check RayTwin against itself. Such a self-check is an “inverse crime” and gives results that are too good. Both simulators use the same ITU formulas and the same geometry. Thus the result is only an upper bound of the real quality.

4.10 Service quality Release 0.2

A radio map tells coverage and interference. SON also needs to know what the UEs get: data rate, cell load and number of handovers. The service-quality layer of release 0.2 calculates these values from the radio map. It runs after the ray trace and costs little. Most what-if questions need no new trace (Figure 6). Figure 14 shows the steps:

  1. A scenario places UEs with traffic demand and trajectories, with a seed (UC-11).
  2. Each UE selects the serving cell with the highest RSRP plus CIO (EQ-17).
  3. The cell loads and the SINR depend on each other: a loaded neighbor cell causes more interference. RayTwin repeats the calculation until the loads stop changing (EQ-18, EQ-20).
  4. The SINR gives the peak data rate of each UE (EQ-19). The load gives the share that each UE gets (EQ-21).
  5. Along each trajectory, RayTwin filters the RSRP and applies the handover rule (EQ-22).

The result is a time series for each UE and each cell. A viewer can replay it. The layer ranks scenarios. It does not predict the absolute throughput of one UE, because the scheduler and the beamforming of each vendor are different (FR-49).

4.11 Plugins Release 0.2 Release 0.3

From release 0.2, a Python package can add a plugin to RayTwin. A plugin can be a data reader, a service-quality model, a KPI, an exporter or, from release 0.3, a SON policy. Plugin code runs only in the Python client. Workers and the ray-tracing core never run plugin code (BR-09). Thus a plugin cannot change the determinism of the ray trace.

The GPU needs antenna patterns and materials (release 0.3). These enter the snapshot as data tables with a hash, not as code. Each plugin declares its name, its version and its API version. These values are part of the result id, so RayTwin never uses a cached result of another plugin version.

4.12 Plan history and rollback Release 0.2

Each edit of a plan is a configuration delta: add, remove or change a transmitter, or change a RAN parameter. A delta makes a new snapshot or a new RAN config with a new hash. It never changes a stored document. A named reference keeps the history of a plan as a log. RayTwin only appends to the log. A rollback appends a new entry that points to an earlier pair (snapshot, RAN config). It deletes nothing, and it uses the stored results again (Figure 16).

4.13 Closed SON loop with a transmitter cache Release 0.3

In release 0.3, a SON policy written in Python proposes configuration changes. RayTwin evaluates each candidate on the twin and returns KPIs. The loop continues until the policy stops or the evaluation budget ends. RayTwin acts as the “environment” of the policy, in the same way as in reinforcement learning.

RayTwin stores the radio map of each transmitter under a key that depends only on the inputs of that transmitter. A tilt change of 3 cells then re-traces 3 transmitters, not all 50. At the end, RayTwin evaluates the best candidates again with another seed. If the ranking changes, the policy can be taking advantage of simulation noise or of a model defect.

UC-01 — Prepare a scene and create a snapshot Release 0.1

Priority: Must Frequency: Each time the scene or another input changes Requirements: FR-01, FR-02, FR-03, FR-04, NFR-19 Status: Draft

Description

The planning engineer collects every input of a calculation into one snapshot. The inputs are the 3D scene, the material field, the bands, the transmitters and the solver settings. The snapshot id then identifies the calculation. Any later job, on any machine, gives the same result for the same id.

Actors

  • Primary actor: Planning engineer.
  • Secondary actors: None.
  • Systems involved: Object store.

Preconditions

  • The scene exists as Mitsuba XML with PLY meshes on local disk, on S3 or on MinIO. From release 0.3, the scene can also come from UC-16.
  • The planning engineer can read the scene location and write to the snapshot location.
  • The RayTwin Python package is installed.

Postconditions

  • On success: the manifest of the snapshot is in the object store. The planning engineer has the snapshot id. No existing object changed.
  • On failure: RayTwin wrote no manifest. The planning engineer has the reason.

Main flow

  1. Planning engineerOpens a store at the snapshot location.
  2. Planning engineerLoads the scene from its storage location.
  3. SystemReads the XML file and the meshes. Calculates the content hash of the scene.
  4. Planning engineerSelects the material field: the ITU prior of the material classes, or a stored field version.
  5. Planning engineerDefines the bands, with center frequency and bandwidth.
  6. Planning engineerDefines the transmitters, with position, azimuth, tilt, bands and antenna array.
  7. Planning engineerDefines the solver settings: rays per transmitter, maximum depth and seed.
  8. Planning engineerAdds the coordinate reference system (CRS) of the scene, if MDT positions will be used later.
  9. SystemWrites the manifest as a new object. Calculates the snapshot id from the manifest without its storage locations.
  10. SystemReturns the snapshot id.

User flow

Alternative flows

UC-01-A1 — The same snapshot exists already

Starts at step 9. A manifest with the same snapshot id is in the store.

  1. The system writes nothing.
  2. The flow continues at step 10 with the stored snapshot id.
UC-01-A2 — The planning engineer stops before the write

Starts at any step before step 9. The planning engineer stops the script.

  1. The system writes nothing.
  2. The use case ends without a snapshot.
UC-01-E1 — The scene contains an element that RayTwin does not support

Starts at step 3. The XML file contains an element outside the supported subset.

  1. The system stops the load. It names the element and its position in the file. It does not ignore the element.
  2. The use case ends without a snapshot.
UC-01-E2 — Stored data does not agree with its hash

Starts at step 3 or step 4. An input that a manifest refers to has another content than its hash says.

  1. The system reports the mismatch and the storage location.
  2. The use case ends without a snapshot.
UC-01-E3 — The object store refuses access or does not answer

Starts at step 2 or step 9.

  1. The system reports the storage error and the storage location.
  2. The use case ends without a snapshot.
UC-01-E4 — An input value is not valid

Starts at steps 5 to 8.

  1. The system names the input field and the reason.
  2. The planning engineer corrects the value. The flow continues at the step of that field.

Notes

  • Business rules. BR-02 (snapshot id), BR-03 (create-only writes, hash check).
  • Good to know. A new material-field version changes the snapshot id but keeps the scene hash. The ray-tracing inputs and the radio parameters are separate. Put the EPRE, the noise figure and the UE gain into the RAN config (UC-03), not into the snapshot.
  • Deliberately excluded. In releases 0.1 and 0.2, RayTwin reads only Mitsuba XML with PLY meshes. Other formats need an offline conversion until UC-16 (release 0.3).

UC-02 — Calculate a 3D radio map Release 0.1

Priority: Must Frequency: For each snapshot to study Requirements: FR-06, FR-09, FR-10, FR-11, NFR-01, NFR-03, NFR-04, NFR-09, NFR-12 Status: Draft

Description

The planning engineer calculates the average path gain in each voxel of a 3D grid, for each transmitter and each band of a snapshot. This radio map is the base of every coverage value: RSRP, SINR and the best server come from it (UC-03). One ray trace serves all bands.

Actors

  • Primary actor: Planning engineer.
  • Secondary actors: None.
  • Systems involved: Object store. In distributed mode, also the systems of UC-07.

Preconditions

  • The snapshot exists (UC-01).
  • The machine has a CPU. A Vulkan GPU on Linux or Windows is optional.

Postconditions

  • On success: a radio-map result exists. It holds path gain for 1 W of transmit power as [band, transmitter, z, y, x], and the segment count as [transmitter, z, y, x]. Its attributes give the grid, the band ids, the transmitter ids, the CRS, the snapshot id and the job id.
  • On failure: no result exists. The planning engineer has the reason.

Main flow

  1. Planning engineerCreates a radio-map job with the snapshot id and a voxel grid. The default voxel size is 5 m.
  2. Planning engineerRuns the job in library mode.
  3. SystemSelects the backend in the order of Figure 10. Records the reason for each skipped backend.
  4. SystemTraces the rays one time for all bands of the snapshot.
  5. SystemAdds the power of each ray segment to the voxels that the segment crosses, for each band.
  6. SystemAdds a warning for each material that it uses outside its ITU validity range.
  7. SystemReturns the radio map with the segment count of each voxel.
  8. Planning engineerReads the path gain. Uses only ray-sufficient voxels for comparisons.

User flow

Alternative flows

UC-02-A1 — The planning engineer forces a backend

Starts at step 3. The configuration names one backend.

  1. The system uses that backend.
  2. The flow continues at step 4.
UC-02-A2 — No suitable GPU

Starts at step 3. The machine has no Vulkan GPU, or the GPU has no ray query, no 64-bit floating point or no 64-bit atomics. On macOS, this is always the case in release 0.1.

  1. The system records the missing feature for each skipped backend.
  2. The system uses the next backend. The CPU is the last one.
  3. The flow continues at step 4. The result is within 10−4 of a GPU result.
UC-02-A3 — A measurement plane in place of a voxel grid

Starts at step 1. The planning engineer needs values on one flat plane, for example to compare with another tool.

  1. The planning engineer gives a measurement plane with its height and grid step.
  2. The flow continues at step 2.
UC-02-A4 — The job runs in distributed mode

Starts at step 2. The area or the ray count is too large for one machine.

  1. The planning engineer submits the job to the distributed service.
  2. The flow continues in UC-07. The result is the same as in library mode.
UC-02-E1 — The planning engineer stops the run

Starts at step 4 or step 5.

  1. The system gives no result.
  2. The use case ends without a radio map. A new run with the same inputs gives the result that the first run would have given (BR-06).
UC-02-E2 — The snapshot cannot be read

Starts at step 2. The snapshot does not exist, the object store refuses access, or a hash does not agree.

  1. The system reports the reason and the storage location.
  2. The use case ends without a radio map.

Notes

  • Business rules. BR-01 (ray-sufficient voxels), BR-06 (same seed, same rays).
  • Good to know. The noise of a ray-sufficient voxel is approximately 0.4 dB (EQ-07). To halve the noise, use four times the rays. A 1 km × 1 km × 60 m area with 5 m voxels needs approximately 1.92 MB for each transmitter and band (EQ-08). RayTwin does not write empty storage chunks.
  • Good to know. Areas deep behind buildings can show a signal that is too low, because releases 0.1 to 0.3 have no diffraction (C-13).
  • Deliberately excluded. Diffraction and diffuse scattering (FR-43). FR2 bands (FR-44).

UC-03 — Read RSRP, SINR and the best server with a RAN config Release 0.1

Priority: Must Frequency: Each time a radio parameter changes. Often many times for one radio map Requirements: FR-05, FR-07, NFR-23 Status: Draft

Description

The planning engineer converts the path-gain radio map into the values that a phone reports: RSRP, and from it SINR and the best server. A RAN config holds the radio parameters. A change of these parameters needs no new ray trace, so the planning engineer can try many values quickly.

Actors

  • Primary actor: Planning engineer.
  • Secondary actors: None.
  • Systems involved: Object store.

Preconditions

  • A radio map exists for the band (UC-02).
  • The planning engineer knows the EPRE of the synchronization signal of each cell and band (A-02).

Postconditions

  • On success: the RAN config is stored with its hash. The planning engineer has RSRP in dBm as [transmitter, z, y, x], SINR, and the serving cell with its RSRP as [z, y, x]. The snapshot id did not change.
  • On failure: the planning engineer has no values and has the reason.

Main flow

  1. Planning engineerWrites the RAN config: EPRE for each cell and band, cell on/off state, UE gain, body loss and noise figure.
  2. SystemStores the RAN config as a new object and returns its hash.
  3. Planning engineerAsks for RSRP at one band, with the RAN config.
  4. SystemApplies the measurement operator (EQ-14). It adds the EPRE and the UE gain, subtracts the body loss, rounds to 1 dB and limits the value to the reporting range.
  5. SystemReturns RSRP for each transmitter and voxel.
  6. Planning engineerAsks for the best server at the band.
  7. SystemReturns the serving cell and its RSRP for each voxel.
  8. Planning engineerAsks for SINR at the band.
  9. SystemAdds the interference from the cells on the same carrier frequency, and the thermal noise. Returns SINR for each voxel (EQ-03).

User flow

Alternative flows

UC-03-A1 — The planning engineer tries another radio parameter

Starts after step 9. For example, the EPRE of one cell increases by 3 dB, or one cell is off.

  1. The planning engineer derives a new RAN config with the changed value.
  2. The flow continues at step 2. The snapshot id and the radio map stay the same.
UC-03-A2 — A cell is off

Starts at step 4. The RAN config marks a cell as off.

  1. The system leaves that cell out of RSRP, best server and interference.
  2. The flow continues at step 5.
UC-03-E1 — The RAN config has no EPRE for a cell of the band

Starts at step 4.

  1. The system names the cell and the band that have no EPRE.
  2. The use case ends without values. The planning engineer can correct the RAN config and start again at step 1.

Notes

  • Business rules. BR-04 (RAN changes need no trace), BR-05 (RSRP, not RSS).
  • Good to know. Use the configured EPRE of the synchronization signal, not the total cell power divided by the subcarriers. Assume that the operator boosts the synchronization signal by 3 dB. The second method then gives a value that is 3 dB too low in the full cell (EQ-14).
  • Good to know. The RSRP, SINR and service-quality values have the identity (job id, RAN config hash).

UC-04 — Calculate channel impulse responses per antenna element Release 0.1

Priority: Must Frequency: For each set of receiver points Requirements: FR-08, NFR-02, NFR-04 Status: Draft

Description

The SON researcher gets the channel between each transmitter antenna element and each receiver antenna element, at receiver points that the researcher selects. Link-level tasks need this result: beam selection, delay spread and precoding. Unlike the radio map, the CIR keeps the phase and the delay of each path.

Actors

  • Primary actor: SON researcher.
  • Secondary actors: Planning engineer, for the same goal.
  • Systems involved: Object store.

Preconditions

  • The snapshot exists (UC-01). Its transmitter arrays have at most 64T64R.
  • The SON researcher has a list of receiver points and a UE antenna array with at most 4 antennas.

Postconditions

  • On success: a paths result exists. It holds the amplitudes a[rx, rx_ant, tx, tx_ant, path, band] and the delays τ[rx, rx_ant, tx, tx_ant, path]. It also holds the angles, the interaction types and a validity mask for each element pair. The SON researcher can read it as NumPy arrays.
  • On failure: no result exists. The SON researcher has the reason.

Main flow

  1. SON researcherGives the receiver points and the UE antenna array.
  2. SON researcherCreates a paths job for the snapshot, in per-element mode (the default).
  3. SystemLaunches rays one time from the center of each transmitter array. Collects the surface sequences that reach the receivers as candidates.
  4. SystemRemoves duplicate candidates.
  5. SystemFor each pair of antenna elements, finds the exact path of each candidate with the image method (EQ-10). Checks that no segment is blocked.
  6. SystemCalculates the delay, the angles, the polarization and the element pattern of each path, for each band.
  7. SystemReturns a, τ and the validity mask. A path has the same index for all element pairs.
  8. SON researcherReads the CIR, or calculates the CFR at the sub-frequencies of a band (EQ-11).

User flow

Alternative flows

UC-04-A1 — Synthetic mode

Starts at step 2. The SON researcher selects synthetic mode for speed. This is correct when each receiver is farther from the array than the Fraunhofer distance (EQ-12).

  1. The system traces from the center of each array only.
  2. The system calculates the response of each element with a phase shift.
  3. The flow continues at step 7.
UC-04-A2 — Large array: rays also from the corners

Starts at step 3. The array is large, and a path can exist only for the elements at its edge.

  1. The SON researcher selects the extra launch from the 4 corners of the transmitter array.
  2. The system joins the candidates of the center and of the corners. The launch cost is 5 times, not the number of elements.
  3. The flow continues at step 4.
UC-04-A3 — The job runs in distributed mode

Starts at step 2.

  1. The SON researcher submits the job to the distributed service. The coordinator splits it by transmitter and group of receivers.
  2. The flow continues in UC-07.
UC-04-E1 — The SON researcher stops the run

Starts at steps 3 to 6.

  1. The system gives no result.
  2. The use case ends without a CIR.
UC-04-E2 — The snapshot cannot be read

Starts at step 2.

  1. The system reports the reason and the storage location.
  2. The use case ends without a CIR.

Notes

  • Good to know. One link with 64T64R, 4 UE antennas and 100 paths needs approximately 200 KB for each band (EQ-13). Thus RayTwin calculates the CIR only at discrete receiver points, not on the full grid.
  • Good to know. Without diffraction and diffuse scattering, the CIR misses energy and late paths deep behind buildings. The delay spread is then too low (C-13).
  • Deliberately excluded. Diffraction and diffuse scattering (FR-43). Moving objects. Doppler from moving objects comes after the MVP. Release 0.1 supports the velocity of the transmitter and the receiver.

UC-05 — View results in 3D and export them Release 0.1

Priority: Must Frequency: After each result Requirements: FR-17, FR-18, FR-28 Status: Draft

Description

UC-07 — Run a job on many workers Release 0.1

Priority: Must Frequency: For each job that is too large for one machine Requirements: FR-12, FR-13, FR-14, FR-15, NFR-06, NFR-07, NFR-08 Status: Draft

Description

The SON researcher sends a job to the distributed service, follows its progress and gets the result. The job can be a radio map, a set of paths or a set of tapes. The result is the same as in library mode. The SON researcher can cancel the job at any time.

Actors

  • Primary actor: SON researcher.
  • Secondary actors: Planning engineer and calibration researcher, for the same goal.
  • Systems involved: Message system, coordinator, workers, object store.

Preconditions

  • The distributed service runs (UC-06).
  • The snapshot is in an object store that the workers can read.
  • The client machine can reach NATS.

Postconditions

  • On success: the job state is “done”. The merged result is in the object store. It is bit-identical to the result of library mode.
  • On failure: the job state is “failed” or “cancelled”. No merged result exists. Parts that workers wrote stay in the object store (BR-03).

Main flow

  1. SON researcherConnects a client to the message system.
  2. SON researcherSubmits the job.
  3. SystemThe coordinator calculates the job id from the job spec and checks the snapshot.
  4. SystemThe coordinator splits the job into tasks and publishes them. A radio map splits by transmitter and ray batch. Paths and tapes split by transmitter and group of receivers or MDT samples.
  5. SystemEach worker pulls one task at a time, calculates it and writes its part to the object store.
  6. SON researcherFollows the job state and the progress.
  7. SystemThe coordinator merges the parts when all tasks are done. It writes the result manifest.
  8. SystemSets the job state to “done” and gives the result location.
  9. SON researcherReads the result in the same way as in library mode.

User flow

Alternative flows

UC-07-A1 — The same job arrives again

Starts at step 3. A job with the same job id exists (BR-07).

  1. If the job runs, the system returns its state. The flow continues at step 6.
  2. If the job is done, the system returns the stored result. The flow continues at step 9.
  3. If the job failed or was cancelled, the system runs it again. The flow continues at step 4.
UC-07-A2 — A worker stops during a task

Starts at step 5. A worker stops before it confirms its task.

  2. The other worker calculates the same part, because the part depends only on the seed.
  3. If the first worker wrote the part already, the second write finds the same content and counts as a success. The coordinator ignores the duplicate “done” event.
  4. The flow continues at step 5. The final result does not change (NFR-07).
UC-07-A3 — The SON researcher cancels the job

Starts at step 5 or step 6.

  1. The SON researcher sends a cancel request.
  2. The coordinator sets the job state to “cancelled” and removes the waiting tasks of the job.

  4. The use case ends without a result.
UC-07-E1 — A task fails permanently

Starts at step 5. A task fails at each delivery.

  1. The message system stops the delivery after 5 attempts.
  2. The coordinator sets the job state to “failed”, with the reason.

UC-07-E2 — An input is missing or does not agree with its hash

Starts at step 3 or step 5.

  • Business rules. BR-03 (create-only), BR-06 (seed), BR-07 (job id).
  • Good to know. Messages carry only references, never large data. Large data goes through the object store.

UC-08 — Calibrate the material field from MDT Release 0.1

Priority: Must Frequency: Each time a new set of MDT data is ready Requirements: FR-19, FR-20, FR-21, FR-22, NFR-20 Status: Draft

Description

The calibration researcher adjusts the material field, so that the predicted RSRP agrees with the RSRP in the MDT reports. The result is a new version of the material field. In release 0.1, the MDT is simulated (UC-09), because no real MDT exists yet (C-05).

Actors

  • Primary actor: Calibration researcher.
  • Secondary actors: None.
  • Systems involved: PyTorch, object store. In distributed mode, also the systems of UC-07.

Preconditions

  • A snapshot with a CRS exists (UC-01).
  • A RAN config with the EPRE of each cell exists (UC-03).
  • The MDT samples are in the canonical schema, with positions in scene coordinates and a position confidence.
  • The network configuration data is correct (A-01).

Postconditions

  • On success: a new field version is stored as a new object. A new snapshot id exists. The scene hash did not change. The old field versions stay readable.
  • On failure: no new field version exists. In distributed mode, the intermediate corrections stay in the calibration folder of the run.

Main flow

  1. Calibration researcherReads the MDT samples.
  2. Calibration researcherAsks for the tape of frozen paths to each sample, for one band.
  3. SystemTraces the rays one time with the current field. Returns the tape as NumPy arrays without a copy.
  4. Calibration researcherSets the loss: Huber loss on the RSRP error, weighted by position confidence, plus TV and L2 regularization (EQ-15).
  5. SystemCalculates the predicted RSRP from the tape and the correction δ, through the measurement operator.
  6. SystemCalculates the loss and its gradient. Updates δ with Adam or L-BFGS.
  7. SystemAfter a set number of steps, traces again with the new field. The flow repeats from step 5.
  8. Calibration researcherStops the loop when the loss converges.
  9. Calibration researcherChecks the field on held-out cells and tilts (UC-09).
  10. Calibration researcherCommits the field with a message.
  11. SystemStores the new field version and returns the new snapshot id.

User flow

Alternative flows

UC-08-A1 — Tapes in distributed mode

Starts at step 3 or step 7. The area or the number of samples is too large for one machine.

  1. The system runs a tape job on the workers, split by transmitter and group of MDT samples (UC-07).
  2. Before each new trace, the system writes the current δ as a new object in the calibration folder of the run.
  3. The calibration researcher reads the tapes as NumPy arrays. The flow continues at step 4 or step 5. The tapes are bit-identical to the tapes of library mode.
UC-08-A2 — MDT from two or more bands

Starts at step 2.

  1. The calibration researcher asks for tapes at each band. One correction δ serves all bands.
  2. The flow continues at step 3. Several bands help to separate the two material parameters εr and σ (EQ-04).
UC-08-E1 — An MDT sample has no value in a required column

Starts at step 1.

  1. The system refuses the sample and gives the reason.
  2. The flow continues at step 1 with the remaining samples.
UC-08-E2 — The field does not pass the held-out check

Starts at step 9.

  1. The calibration researcher does not commit the field.
  2. The calibration researcher reviews the prior, the regularization and the grid step.
  3. The use case ends without a new field version.
UC-08-E3 — The calibration researcher stops the loop

Starts at steps 3 to 8.

  1. The system stores no new field version.
  2. The use case ends.

Notes

  • Business rules. BR-05 (compare by RSRP), BR-08 (split by cell and tilt), BR-11 (weight by position confidence).
  • Good to know. With δ = 0, the result is bit-identical to the ITU materials of the classes. The Fresnel formulas exist two times, in the RayTwin core and in PyTorch. A CI test makes sure that they agree within 10−6.
  • Risk. A wrong network configuration, a wrong EPRE or a poor MDT position can make the field absorb an error (A-01, A-02). The field can then fit the data and still be wrong after a tilt change.
  • Deliberately excluded. Continuous calibration from live MDT (FR-42). Joint calibration of the network configuration and the materials comes after the MVP.

UC-12 — Evaluate the service quality of a configuration Release 0.2

Priority: Must Frequency: For each candidate configuration Requirements: FR-27, FR-28, NFR-13, NFR-14 Status: Draft

Description

The SON researcher estimates what the UEs get under one network configuration. This includes the serving cell, the SINR, the data rate, the cell load and the handovers. The result is a time series. KPIs from it rank the configurations, for load balancing, coverage and capacity, mobility robustness, energy saving and capacity planning.

Actors

  • Primary actor: SON researcher.
  • Secondary actors: Planning engineer, for the same goal.
  • Systems involved: Object store.

Preconditions

  • A radio map exists for the bands (UC-02).
  • A RAN config exists, with CIO, handover parameters, number of resource blocks and subcarrier spacing.
  • A scenario exists (UC-11).

Postconditions

  • On success: the run is stored as a time series. Its id is the hash of the job id, the RAN config hash, the scenario and the plugin identities. The SON researcher has the KPIs.
  • On failure: no run is stored. The SON researcher has the reason.

Main flow

  1. SON researcherSelects the radio map, the RAN config and the scenario.
  2. SON researcherStarts the evaluation.
  3. SystemCalculates the RSRP at each UE position with the measurement operator.
  4. SystemConnects each UE to the cell with the highest RSRP plus CIO (EQ-17). A UE below the minimum RSRP is in outage.
  5. SystemCalculates the cell loads and the SINR together. It starts at load 0 and repeats until the loads stop changing (EQ-18, EQ-20).
  6. SystemCalculates the data rate and the throughput of each UE (EQ-19, EQ-21).
  7. SystemFor each time step of the trajectories, filters the RSRP and applies the handover rule (EQ-22).
  8. SystemStores the time series and returns the run id.
  9. SON researcherReads the KPIs, for example the 10th-percentile throughput, the highest cell load, the handovers for each UE and the ping-pongs.

UC-17 — Run a closed SON loop Release 0.3

Priority: Must Frequency: For each SON study Requirements: FR-38, FR-39, FR-40, NFR-16 Status: Draft

Description

The SON researcher tests a SON algorithm on the twin before it touches the live network. Examples are load balancing, mobility robustness, coverage and capacity, and energy saving. The policy proposes candidates. RayTwin evaluates each candidate and returns KPIs. The loop runs until the policy stops or the budget ends.

Actors

  • Primary actor: SON researcher.
  • Secondary actors: None.
  • Systems involved: Object store. Optional: the systems of distributed mode (UC-07).

Preconditions

  • A baseline (snapshot, RAN config) and a scenario exist (UC-11).
  • A SON policy plugin is installed, or the SON researcher uses the built-in grid search.

Postconditions

  • On success: the SON researcher has the history of the evaluated candidates with their KPIs, and the report of the seed check.
  • On failure: the loop stopped before the end. The SON researcher has the reason.

Main flow

  1. SON researcherSelects the policy, the baseline, the scenario and the budget, for example 200 evaluations.
  2. SON researcherStarts the loop.
  3. SystemGives the history to the policy and gets candidate configuration deltas.
  4. SystemSorts each delta. A RAN change needs only a service-quality evaluation. A change of tilt, azimuth, antenna or a new site needs a new trace of the changed transmitters.
  5. SystemTraces the changed transmitters. Uses the cached results of the other transmitters.
  6. SystemEvaluates the service quality of each candidate, with the same scenario seed and ray seed (UC-12).
  7. SystemAdds the KPIs to the history. It never evaluates the same candidate two times.
  8. SystemRepeats from step 3 until the policy stops or the budget ends.
  9. SystemEvaluates the best candidates again with another seed. Reports whether the ranking changed.
  10. SON researcherReads the history and the report of the seed check.

User flow

Alternative flows

Starts at step 3.

Notes

  • Business rules. BR-04, BR-06, BR-10.
  • Example: load balancing. The policy raises the CIO of the overloaded cell by 1 dB at each step, until the highest load is below 0.8. No step needs a new ray trace.

  • Good to know. The only built-in policy is a grid search, as a reference. RayTwin needs no reinforcement-learning library. A wrapper for such a library can be a plugin.

Appendix A — Formulas explained

This appendix gives each formula that a RayTwin user meets. Each formula has five parts:

  1. the question that it answers,
  2. the formula,
  3. a table of its symbols with their units,
  4. an explanation in plain words,
  5. a numerical example.

The examples come from the TR. Many examples use the same small scene, so you can follow the numbers from one formula to the next.

How to read decibels. Radio engineers write power ratios in decibels (dB): \( x_{\mathrm{dB}} = 10\log_{10} x \). +3 dB is approximately 2 times the power. +10 dB is 10 times. +20 dB is 100 times. A power in dBm is a power relative to 1 mW: 0 dBm = 1 mW, 30 dBm = 1 W. In decibels, a product becomes a sum. Thus a gain adds and a loss subtracts.

A.1 Signal strength

EQ-01 — Free-space path gain and path loss

How much power arrives at a distance d, when nothing blocks the wave?

\[ g_{\mathrm{FS}}(d) = \left(\frac{\lambda}{4\pi d}\right)^{2}, \qquad \mathrm{PL}_{\mathrm{dB}}(d) = 20\log_{10}\frac{4\pi d}{\lambda}, \qquad \lambda = \frac{c}{f} \]
SymbolMeaningUnit
\( g_{\mathrm{FS}} \)Free-space path gain: received power divided by transmitted power, for isotropic antennasnone (ratio)
\( \mathrm{PL}_{\mathrm{dB}} \)Path loss, the inverse of the path gaindB
\( d \)Distance between the two antennasm
\( \lambda \)Wavelengthm
\( c \)Speed of light, \( 3\cdot10^{8} \)m/s
\( f \)FrequencyHz

In plain words. The power spreads over a sphere around the antenna. The surface of the sphere grows with the square of the distance. Thus the received power falls with the square of the distance. A higher frequency has a shorter wavelength, and the loss at the same distance is higher. Ray tracing applies this rule to each path. Each wall hit then multiplies the power by a material factor (EQ-05).

Example: path loss at 3.5 GHz
  1. \( \lambda = 3\cdot10^{8} / 3.5\cdot10^{9} \approx 0.0857 \) m, that is 8.57 cm.
  2. At \( d = 100 \) m: \( \mathrm{PL} = 20\log_{10}(4\pi\cdot100/0.0857) \approx 83.3 \) dB.
  3. At \( d = 500 \) m: \( \mathrm{PL} \approx 97.3 \) dB.
  4. Five times the distance adds \( 20\log_{10}5 \approx 14 \) dB.

Used in: section 4.2, EQ-08.

EQ-02 — RSRP from the link budget

What RSRP does a phone measure, when you know the transmit power, the antenna gains and the path loss?

\[ \mathrm{RSRP}\,[\mathrm{dBm}] = P_{\mathrm{RE}} + G_t + G_r - \mathrm{PL} \]
SymbolMeaningUnit
\( \mathrm{RSRP} \)Reference signal received powerdBm
\( P_{\mathrm{RE}} \)Transmit power of one resource element (one subcarrier in one symbol), the EPREdBm

In plain words. In decibels, gains add and losses subtract. RSRP is the power of one resource element, not of the full band. Thus the calculation starts from the power of one resource element, not from the total power of the cell.

Example: RSRP

  1. \( P_{\mathrm{RE}} = 46 - 10\log_{10}3276 \approx 10.85 \) dBm.
  2. \( \mathrm{RSRP} \approx 10.85 + 17 + 0 - 120 \approx -92.2 \) dBm.

EQ-03 — SINR and thermal noise

How clean is the wanted signal, compared with the interference and the noise?

In plain words. SINR compares the wanted signal with everything that disturbs it. Only cells on the same carrier frequency interfere. The noise grows with the width of one resource element and with the noise figure of the receiver. Add the powers as linear values in mW, not as dB values.

  1. Noise in one resource element at 30 kHz with NF = 7 dB: \( -174 + 10\log_{10}(30\,000) + 7 \approx -122.2 \) dBm.

EQ-04 — Electrical parameters of a material (ITU-R P.2040)

Which numbers describe a material for a radio wave at a given frequency?

\[ \eta = \varepsilon_r - j\,\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0\,\omega}, \qquad \varepsilon_r = a\,f_{\mathrm{GHz}}^{\,b}, \qquad \sigma = c\,f_{\mathrm{GHz}}^{\,d} \]

In plain words. Two numbers describe a material for a radio wave. \( \varepsilon_r \) tells how strongly it reacts to the field. \( \sigma \) tells how much power it absorbs.   2. \( \varepsilon_r = 5.24 \) and \( \sigma = 0.0462\cdot3.5^{0.7822} \approx 0.123 \) S/m.

EQ-05 — Fresnel reflection coefficients

Which part of a wave reflects from a wall?

\[ r_{\perp} = \frac{\cos\vartheta - \sqrt{\eta - \sin^{2}\vartheta}}{\cos\vartheta + \sqrt{\eta - \sin^{2}\vartheta}}, \qquad r_{\parallel} = \frac{\eta\cos\vartheta - \sqrt{\eta - \sin^{2}\vartheta}}{\eta\cos\vartheta + \sqrt{\eta - \sin^{2}\vartheta}} \]

In plain words. A reflection coefficient tells which part of the wave amplitude reflects. Its square tells which part of the power reflects. The rest goes into the wall. EQ-06 — Material field with a correction

How does RayTwin change the materials of an area without changing their classes?

\[ \varepsilon_r(\mathbf{x}, f) = 1 + \big(a_{c}\, f^{\,b_{c}} - 1\big)\,e^{\delta_\varepsilon(\mathbf{x})}, \qquad \sigma(\mathbf{x}, f) = c_{c}\, f^{\,d_{c}}\, e^{\delta_\sigma(\mathbf{x})}, \qquad c = c(\mathbf{x}) \]

\( a_c, b_c, c_c, d_c \)ITU parameters of that class (EQ-04)—

In plain words. The class gives the start value. The correction multiplies it. With \( \delta = 0 \), the value is exactly the ITU value. The exponential keeps \( \varepsilon_r \) above 1 and \( \sigma \) above 0 for any \( \delta \). The correction does not depend on the frequency, so one field serves all bands. A step of the correction has the same relative effect on every material.

Example: interpolation in a grid box
  1. The grid step is 4 m. The hit point is at (1, 2, 3) m from the corner of its box, so its relative position is (0.25, 0.5, 0.75).
  2. The weight of the corner (1, 1, 1) is \( 0.25 \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.75 \approx 0.094 \).
  3. The weight of the corner (0, 0, 1) is \( 0.75 \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.75 \approx 0.281 \).
  4. The 8 weights add up to 1. Thus each hit point changes the gradient of exactly 8 grid points.

A.3 How the radio map is estimated

EQ-07 — Monte Carlo noise

How precise is the value of one voxel?

\[ \frac{\Delta S}{S} \approx \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \]
SymbolMeaningUnit
\( \Delta S / S \)Relative error of the value in the voxelnone
\( n \)Number of ray segments that cross the voxelnone

In plain words. Each ray is a random sample. More samples give a better average. The error falls with the square root of the number of samples. Thus half the noise needs four times the rays. Areas far away or behind buildings get fewer rays, so their values have more noise.

Example: the ray-sufficient voxel
  1. A voxel with \( n = 100 \) segments has a relative error of approximately \( 1/\sqrt{100} = 10\,\% \).
  2. 10 % in power is approximately 0.4 dB.
  3. For this reason, comparisons use only voxels with at least 100 segments (BR-01).

Used in: section 4.2, UC-02.

EQ-08 — Track-length estimator

How does RayTwin turn many rays into the average path gain of a voxel?

\[ \bar{S}_v = \frac{1}{V}\sum_{i} P_i\,\ell_{i,v}, \qquad g_v = \frac{\lambda^{2}}{4\pi P_t}\,\bar{S}_v \]
SymbolMeaningUnit
\( \bar{S}_v \)Average power flux density in voxel vW/m²

\( P_i \)Power that ray i carries. It starts at \( P_t G_t / N \) and loses power at each hitW

In plain words. A ray that stays longer inside a voxel adds more (Figure 3). The sum over all rays, divided by the volume, gives the average power density. In free space, it gives the value of EQ-01.

Example: the size of a radio map
  1. An area of 1 km × 1 km × 60 m with 5 m voxels has 200 × 200 × 12 = 480 000 voxels.
  2. In 32-bit floating point, this is approximately 1.92 MB for each pair (transmitter, band).
  3. 50 transmitters and 4 bands need approximately 384 MB. Thus RayTwin stores the map in chunks and does not write empty chunks.

EQ-09 — Weight for each band

How can one ray trace give a correct result for several bands?

\[ P_{i,b} = \frac{P_{t,b}\,G_{t,b}(\theta_i,\varphi_i)}{N}\prod_{k}\frac{\lvert c_{k,b}\rvert^{2}}{q_k} \]
SymbolMeaningUnit
\( P_{i,b} \)Power of ray i at band bW
\( P_{t,b} \)Transmit power at band bW
\( G_{t,b}(\theta_i,\varphi_i) \)Antenna gain at band b in the start direction of ray inone

\( c_{k,b} \)Coefficient of the k-th hit of the ray at band b (reflection or transmission)none

In plain words. RayTwin selects reflection or transmission one time, at a reference frequency. Another band has slightly different coefficients. The weight \( |c|^2/q \) corrects the power for that band. On average over many rays, each band gets its correct share of power.

  1. A ray hits a concrete wall. At the reference frequency, \( |r|^2 = 0.16 \) and \( |t|^2 = 0.84 \). The trace selects reflection with the probability \( q = 0.16 \).
  2. At band b, \( |r_b|^2 = 0.20 \). The reflected power of the ray at band b gets the weight \( 0.20/0.16 = 1.25 \).
  3. On average, the reflected share at band b is then 0.20, which is correct.

A.4 Paths and the channel

EQ-10 — Image method

Where exactly does a reflected path touch the wall?

\[ \mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} - 2\,\big((\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_0)\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}\big)\,\hat{\mathbf{n}} \]
SymbolMeaningUnit
\( \mathbf{x} \)The point to mirror, for example the transmitterm
\( \mathbf{x}' \)The mirror image of \( \mathbf{x} \) (the virtual source)m
\( \mathbf{p}_0 \)Any point on the plane of the wallm
\( \hat{\mathbf{n}} \)Unit normal of the wallnone

In plain words. The formula mirrors a point across a flat wall. The straight line from the mirror image to the receiver crosses the wall at the reflection point. The length of the path through the wall equals the length of that straight line. For several reflections, RayTwin mirrors again across each wall in order. The path is valid only when each reflection point is on its wall and nothing blocks a segment.

Example: one reflection on a wall (Figure 4)
  1. The wall is the plane x = 10 m, with \( \hat{\mathbf{n}} = (1, 0, 0) \). Tx = (2, 0, 10). Rx = (4, 30, 1.5).
  2. The image of Tx is Tx′ = (18, 0, 10).
  3. The line Tx′–Rx crosses the wall at P = (10, 17.14, 5.14).
  4. The reflected path is \( |\mathrm{Tx}' - \mathrm{Rx}| = \sqrt{14^2 + 30^2 + 8.5^2} \approx 34.18 \) m long. The LoS path is \( \sqrt{2^2 + 30^2 + 8.5^2} \approx 31.24 \) m.
  5. The angle of incidence: \( \cos\vartheta = 14/34.18 \), so \( \vartheta \approx 65.8^\circ \).

Used in: section 4.3, UC-04.

EQ-11 — Channel impulse response (CIR) and frequency response (CFR)

Which echoes reach the receiver, and how does the channel change with frequency?

\[ h(\tau) = \sum_{n=1}^{N} a_n\,\delta(\tau - \tau_n), \qquad H(f) = \sum_{n=1}^{N} a_n\,e^{-j2\pi f \tau_n}, \qquad \tau_n = \frac{L_n}{c} \]
SymbolMeaningUnit
\( h(\tau) \)Channel impulse response: what the receiver gets after a very short pulse—
\( a_n \)Complex amplitude of path n: its strength and its phasenone
\( \tau_n \)Delay of path ns
\( L_n \)Length of path nm
\( \delta(\cdot) \)A pulse at one instant. Here it is not the material correction of EQ-06—
\( H(f) \)Channel frequency response at the frequency fnone

In plain words. The CIR lists the echoes that arrive at the receiver, each with its strength, its phase and its delay. The CFR shows the same channel as a function of frequency. When two echoes arrive with opposite phase, they cancel at some frequencies. These dips are called fading. An average-power radio map cannot show them. A radio map needs only \( \sum |a_n|^2 \).

Example: a channel with two paths at 3.5 GHz
  1. LoS path (Figure 4): \( \tau_0 = 31.24/c \approx 104.2 \) ns, \( |a_0| = \lambda/(4\pi\cdot31.24) \approx 2.18\cdot10^{-4} \) (−73.2 dB).
  2. Reflected path, TE polarization assumed: \( \tau_1 \approx 114.0 \) ns, \( |a_1| \approx 0.676 \cdot 2.0\cdot10^{-4} \approx 1.35\cdot10^{-4} \) (−77.4 dB).
  3. \( |H(f)| \) moves between \( |a_0| + |a_1| \approx -69.0 \) dB and \( |a_0| - |a_1| \approx -81.6 \) dB. The fading dips are approximately 12.5 dB deep.
  4. Two dips are \( 1/\Delta\tau \approx 1/9.79\,\mathrm{ns} \approx 102 \) MHz apart. Thus a 100 MHz channel can contain a full dip.

Used in: section 4.3, UC-04.

EQ-14 — Measurement operator

Which value would a phone report at this point?

\[ \widehat{\mathrm{RSRP}}_{c,b}(\mathbf{x}) = Q\Big[\mathrm{EPRE}_{c,b} + 10\log_{10} g_{c,b}(\mathbf{x}) + G_{\mathrm{UE}} - L_{\mathrm{body}}\Big] \]

  1. A cell transmits 46 dBm over 3276 subcarriers. If you assume equal power on all subcarriers, the EPRE is \( 46 - 10\log_{10}3276 \approx 10.85 \) dBm.
  2. Assume that the operator boosts the synchronization signal by 3 dB. The real EPRE is then 13.85 dBm.
  3. An RSRP derived from the total power is then 3 dB too low at every point of this cell.
  4. Calibration would lower the material loss around the cell to compensate. The field would be wrong, and more wrong after a tilt change.

EQ-15 — Calibration objective

Which correction δ agrees best with the measurements, and stays physical?

\[ \hat{\delta} = \arg\min_{\delta}\ \sum_{m} w_m\,\rho_\kappa\!\left(\mathrm{RSRP}_m - \widehat{\mathrm{RSRP}}_m(\delta)\right) + \lambda_1\,\mathrm{TV}(\delta) + \lambda_2\,\lVert\delta\rVert_2^{2} \]

  2. A sample with an error of 2 dB adds \( \tfrac12 \cdot 2^2 = 2 \), as a normal square does.
  3. A sample with an error of 10 dB, often from a wrong position, adds \( 3\cdot(10 - 1.5) = 25.5 \), not \( \tfrac12\cdot10^2 = 50 \).
  4. Thus the outlier pulls the result much less.

In plain words. The Shannon formula gives the theoretical maximum. A real system reaches only a share \( \alpha \) of it, with a ceiling \( \mathrm{SE}_{\max} \) and a floor \( \mathrm{SINR}_{\min} \). When measured throughput or PM counters exist, RayTwin fits \( \alpha \), \( \mathrm{SE}_{\max} \) and a beamforming gain for each cell. A link-model plugin can replace this formula (UC-14).

Example

With the values above and \( \mathrm{SINR} \approx 6.0 \) dB: \( R \approx 117.3 \) Mbit/s.

Used in: UC-12.

EQ-20 — Cell load

Which share of its resources does a cell need for its UEs?

\[ \rho_c = \min\Big(1,\ \sum_{u:\,c^{*}(u)=c} \frac{d_u}{R_u(\rho)}\Big) \]
SymbolMeaningUnit
\( \rho_c \)Load of cell c, from 0 to 1none
\( d_u \)Traffic demand of UE ubit/s
\( R_u(\rho) \)Peak data rate of UE u (EQ-19). It depends on the loads through the SINRbit/s

In plain words. Each UE needs a share of the cell equal to its demand divided by its peak rate. The load is the sum of these shares. A higher neighbor load lowers the SINR, lowers the rate and raises the load. RayTwin starts from load 0 and repeats until the loads stop changing. The values only grow and stay at or below 1, so the calculation always ends. A cell at load 1 is overloaded. Its extra demand is reported as “not served”.

Used in: UC-12.

EQ-21 — UE throughput

What data rate does a UE really get, when it shares the cell?

\[ T_u = \frac{R_u}{n_c}\ \ (\text{full buffer}), \qquad T_u = R_u\,(1 - \rho_c)\ \ (\text{elastic traffic}) \]
SymbolMeaningUnit
\( T_u \)Throughput of UE ubit/s
\( n_c \)Number of UEs in cell cnone
\( \rho_c \)Load of the serving cell (EQ-20)none

In plain words. With full buffer traffic, each UE always has data, and the UEs share the time equally. With elastic traffic, data arrives in flows, and all active flows share the cell equally. The busier the cell, the less each flow gets.

Example: 25 % more traffic gives 56 % less throughput

Assumption: two symmetric n78 cells, 100 MHz, 273 PRBs, values of EQ-19. For each UE, the serving cell gives −90 dBm and the other cell −96 dBm. The noise is −122.2 dBm in one resource element.

  1. Demand of 80 Mbit/s for each cell. The repeated calculation ends after approximately 15 rounds at \( \rho \approx 0.50 \). Then SINR ≈ 9.0 dB, R ≈ 160.2 Mbit/s and T ≈ 160.2 × (1 − 0.50) ≈ 80.2 Mbit/s.
  2. Demand of 100 Mbit/s (+25 %). The load goes to \( \rho \approx 0.74 \). Then SINR ≈ 7.3 dB, R ≈ 135.6 Mbit/s and T ≈ 35.6 Mbit/s.
  3. The throughput falls by 56 %. The load of each cell lowers the SINR of the other cell, so the effect is much larger than the change of demand.

Used in: UC-12.

EQ-22 — Measurement filter and handover event A3

When does a moving UE change its serving cell?

\[ F_n = (1-a)\,F_{n-1} + a\,M_n, \quad a = 2^{-k/4}; \qquad F^{\mathrm{nb}}_n + O_{fn} + O_{cn} - \mathrm{Hys} > F^{\mathrm{sv}}_n + O_{fp} + O_{cp} + \mathrm{Off}\ \ \text{during TTT} \]

In plain words. The UE smooths its measurements, so a short dip does not start a handover. A handover starts when the neighbor stays better than the serving cell, by the offset plus the hysteresis, for the full time to trigger. Hysteresis and TTT prevent fast handovers back and forth (ping-pong).

Example
  1. Filter coefficients k = 0, 4 and 8 give a = 1, 0.5 and 0.25. With a = 1 there is no smoothing.
  2. Typical values are Hys = 1 dB, Off = 3 dB and TTT = 320 ms.
  3. A return to the old cell within 1 s counts as a ping-pong (default window, A-05).

Used in: UC-12, UC-17.
